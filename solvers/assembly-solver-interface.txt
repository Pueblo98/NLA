Proposed assembly and solver handoff — 6 November 2026
Working specification, not a final report or measured result.

Inputs
- nodes: float array (N, 2), coordinates in metres.
- triangles: integer array (M, 3), zero-based, counter-clockwise.
- boundary_edges: named integer arrays (B, 2), each physical edge once.
  Boundary node sets may additionally be supplied for selecting supports.
- DOF ordering: [ux0, uy0, ux1, uy1, ...].
- Material metadata: E (Pa), nu, plane stress/strain, thickness t (m).
  Values remain unconfirmed; do not silently insert material defaults.
- Constraints: unique DOF indices c and matching prescribed values d_c (m).

Assembly contract
assemble(nodes, triangles, E, nu, *, plane, thickness) -> CSR K (2N, 2N)
An adapter may be needed for the provided routine's actual signature.
Retain the original K and f for reaction recovery.

Loading convention
Use line load q in N/m along the 2D boundary, with global x/y components.
For constant q on a straight edge of length L, add L*q/2 to each endpoint.
If input instead specifies physical traction tau in Pa, use q = t*tau.
Do not apply thickness twice. Record whether loads are total or per thickness.
Keep q and the loaded physical boundary fixed during mesh refinement.

Clarifications for model integration
- Keep applied loads at constrained DOFs in the original f. Prescribing d_c
  removes those equations from the solve, not those loads from bookkeeping.
  The reaction is r_c = K[c,:] d - f_c. Deleting f_c corrupts reactions.
- Plane strain specifies the constitutive assumption, not a mandatory depth.
  For constant out-of-plane depth t, K_e = t*A_e*B.T*D_strain*B and
  q = t*tau are consistent. Unit depth is a reporting convention; do not
  reject other positive depths solely because plane="strain".
  Label total forces versus forces per unit depth explicitly.
- Plane stress remains a proposed baseline assumption pending the supplied
  routine/material data, rather than a confirmed course requirement.

Reduction and recovery
Let free be the complement of c; A = K[free, free].
b = f[free] - K[free, c] d_c.
Solve A d_free = b, then reconstruct the full displacement d.
r = K d - f: r[c] gives support reactions; r[free] is solver residual.
Check total applied force plus support reactions, and their total moment.

Solver comparison proposal for Monday
Small case: LU and Cholesky; larger case: CG and diagonal-PCG.
Use the same reduced system, initial guess and stopping threshold for CG/PCG.
Diagonal-PCG applies z_i = r_i/A_ii; verify positive diagonal and SPD assumptions.
Check convergence status and the true residual independently of solver reports.
Report absolute residual; relative residual is undefined when ||b||_2 = 0.
Separate factorization/preconditioner setup from solve time.
Collect residual histories separately from timing runs to avoid diagnostic overhead.
Dense Cholesky is acceptable only for the explicitly small example.

Outstanding dependency
The three supplied briefs do not include the assembly routine or numerical material
properties. This interface is a proposal to prepare integration, not evidence that
an elastic deformation experiment has been run.

Baseline integration checks (latest geometry proposal)
- Use the 2 by 1 rectangle from Frambuesa's latest mesh specification;
  this supersedes the earlier 1 by 1 suggestion. Left edge clamped.
- With coordinates in metres, the right edge has length 1 m. Constant
  line load q must assemble to total force (1 m)*q, independent of mesh.
- For horizontal q=(Q,0), the applied moment about (0,0) is
  -Q*(1 m)^2/2; reactions must cancel both total force and moment.
- Compare the sum of loaded edge lengths against 1 m to catch duplicated
  or missing edges before solving.
- For zero prescribed displacements and fixed K, doubling f must double d
  and the support reactions to solver tolerance. This checks the linear
  implementation, not the physical validity of large deformations.
- These are proposed acceptance checks, not measured results.
