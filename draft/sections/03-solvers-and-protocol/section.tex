\section{Numerical methods and comparison protocol}
\label{sec:solvers}

Section~\ref{sec:mechanics-matrix} showed that the supported matrix $A$ is
sparse, symmetric and positive definite, with sensitivity measured by
$\kappa_2(A)$. We now use these properties to choose solvers and fix one
protocol for comparing them. Let $N=|F|$ be the number of unknowns, $x_k$
the $k$-th iterate, $e_k=x-x_k$ its error and $r_k=b-Ax_k$ its residual.

\subsection{Direct methods}
A direct method factorizes $A$ into triangular factors: $PA=LU$ with a row
permutation $P$, or, for symmetric positive definite $A$, Cholesky
$A=LL^T$ without pivoting at half the cost~\cite{trefethen-bau}. Dense
factorization costs $O(N^3)$ operations but each later solve only $O(N^2)$;
forming the inverse costs more and is less accurate. In sparse $A$,
elimination creates \emph{fill-in}, nonzeros of the factors where $A$ has
zeros. SciPy's \texttt{splu} limits it with a column ordering (COLAMD) before
LU with partial pivoting~\cite{scipy-splu}; factorization dominates the cost,
and each solve is two triangular substitutions.

\subsection{Stationary iterations}
Iterative methods need only products with $A$. A stationary method
corrects the iterate with the residual, using an easily inverted
approximation $M$ of $A$:
\begin{equation}
 x_{k+1}=x_k+M^{-1}(b-Ax_k),\qquad e_{k+1}=(I-M^{-1}A)\,e_k.
 \label{eq:solvers-splitting}
\end{equation}
It converges for every $x_0$ if and only if the spectral radius
$\rho(I-M^{-1}A)$ is below one~\cite{saad-iterative}. \emph{Jacobi} uses
$M=D_A$, the diagonal of $A$. Its iteration matrix is similar to $I-S$,
where $S=D_A^{-1/2}AD_A^{-1/2}$, so
\begin{equation}
 \rho_J=\max_i\,|1-\lambda_i(S)|.
 \label{eq:solvers-jacobi-radius}
\end{equation}
Positive definiteness only gives $\lambda_i(S)>0$, so Jacobi diverges when
$\lambda_{\max}(S)>2$. \emph{Damped Jacobi}, $M=D_A/\omega$, converges if and
only if $0<\omega<2/\lambda_{\max}(S)$; we use $\omega=0.5$ and check this
bound for each system. \emph{Gauss--Seidel} takes $M$ as the lower triangle of
$A$, including the diagonal, so each sweep is one forward substitution. It
converges for every symmetric positive definite $A$~\cite{saad-iterative}.

\subsection{Krylov methods}
For symmetric positive definite $A$, the solution of $Ax=b$ minimizes
$\phi(y)=\tfrac12y^TAy-b^Ty$, the elastic energy of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:mechanics-energy} minus the work of the loads, and
$r=-\nabla\phi$. \emph{Steepest descent} (SD) moves along $r_k$ to the minimum
of $\phi$ on that line. Each step multiplies its error in the energy norm
$\|e\|_A=(e^TAe)^{1/2}$ by at most $(\kappa-1)/(\kappa+1)$, where
$\kappa=\kappa_2(A)$~\cite{shewchuk-cg}. The \emph{conjugate gradient} (CG)
method instead minimizes $\|e_k\|_A$ over all of $x_0+\mathcal{K}_k$, where
$\mathcal{K}_k=\mathrm{span}\{r_0,Ar_0,\ldots,$ $A^{k-1}r_0\}$ is the Krylov
space. It needs one product with $A$ per iteration and satisfies~\cite{shewchuk-cg}
\begin{equation}
 \|e_k\|_A\leq2\left(\frac{\sqrt\kappa-1}{\sqrt\kappa+1}\right)^k\|e_0\|_A,
 \label{eq:solvers-cg-bound}
\end{equation}
so its iteration count grows with $\sqrt\kappa$ rather than $\kappa$. This
matters because, as for the model Poisson problem, $\kappa$ grows like
$h^{-2}$ on quasi-uniform meshes of size $h$~\cite{elman-silvester-wathen}.

\emph{Diagonally preconditioned CG} (PCG) uses $D_A$ as preconditioner and is
equivalent to CG on $S$, so $\kappa_2(S)$ replaces $\kappa$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:solvers-cg-bound}. If the diagonal is nearly constant, $S$ is
nearly a multiple of $A$ and nothing is gained. Two-dimensional element
stiffness does not scale with element size, so the diagonal varies only with
the number and shape of elements at a node and with material or thickness. \emph{MINRES} minimizes
$\|r_k\|_2$ over $x_0+\mathcal{K}_k$ for symmetric
$A$~\cite{paige-saunders-minres}; \emph{GMRES} does so for general
$A$~\cite{saad-schultz-gmres}, but must store and orthogonalize against all
$k$ basis vectors, costing $O(kN)$ memory and work at step $k$. For symmetric
$A$ both give the same iterates in exact arithmetic, so full GMRES (restart
length $N$) measures the cost of ignoring symmetry. CG, PCG and MINRES
exploit this structure; the other methods are baselines predicted by
$\rho_J$ and $\kappa$.

\subsection{Comparison protocol}
Every method receives the same $A$ and $b$ and starts from $x_0=0$. A
returned vector $\hat x$ is accepted only if its recomputed relative
residual satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \eta(\hat x)=\frac{\|b-A\hat x\|_2}{\|b\|_2}<10^{-8}.
 \label{eq:solvers-residual}
\end{equation}
Native solver flags are recorded but never replace this test, since SciPy's
Krylov solvers stop on internal residual
estimates~\cite{scipy-gmres,scipy-minres}; MINRES gets native tolerance zero
and stops once Eq.~\eqref{eq:solvers-residual} holds. An iteration is
a sweep for the stationary methods, a search direction for SD, CG, PCG and
MINRES, and an inner Arnoldi step for GMRES, which never restarts. The cap is
20{,}000 iterations, and ordinary Jacobi also stops if $\eta>10^{10}$ or
becomes non-finite. Hitting the cap or this divergence guard counts as
failure; the status, iteration count and final $\eta$ are still reported.

Accuracy is the relative error
$\|\hat x-x_{\mathrm{ref}}\|_2/\|x_{\mathrm{ref}}\|_2$ against the sparse LU
solution $x_{\mathrm{ref}}$ of the same system. As $\hat x$ solves the system
with load perturbed by its residual, the standard bound~\cite{trefethen-bau}
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\|\delta x\|_2}{\|x\|_2}\leq\kappa_2(A)\frac{\|\delta b\|_2}{\|b\|_2}
 \label{eq:solvers-perturbation}
\end{equation}
limits this error to $\kappa_2(A)\,\eta$. This \emph{algebraic} error
measures how well one mesh's equations are solved. \emph{Discretization}
error, between finite element and continuum displacements, does not shrink
with the tolerance; we assess it by comparing meshes at matched physical
points under the same physical load.

Times are the best of three wall-clock runs (\texttt{perf\_counter}). Sparse
LU factorization and solve are timed separately. Iterative times include
method-specific setup and stopping checks, but exclude assembly, reduction, eigenvalue diagnostics, the reference solve
and residual histories, which come from a separate run. They compare these
SciPy implementations on one machine, not intrinsic algorithm costs. The
small worked system also compares dense $PA=LU$, the explicit inverse and
Cholesky. The eigenvalues in Eq.~\eqref{eq:mechanics-conditioning} come from
dense \texttt{eigvalsh} for small systems, otherwise from \texttt{eigsh} with
tolerance $10^{-10}$: \texttt{which=\textquotesingle LA\textquotesingle} for $\lambda_{\max}$ and
shift-invert about zero for $\lambda_{\min}$~\cite{scipy-eigsh}. Applied to
$S$, they give $\rho_J$ and the damped-Jacobi bound.

Sections~\ref{sec:square} and~\ref{sec:results} also check that applied
loads and the reactions of Eq.~\eqref{eq:mechanics-reactions} have zero
resultant force and moment, up to rounding, and that $d_C$ equals the
prescribed values. An affine patch test restrains the left edge only
horizontally and the corner at the origin vertically, with uniform
traction $p$ on the
opposite edge; the plane-stress solution $u_x=px/E$, $u_y=-\nu py/E$ is
linear, so the computed nodal values must match it to rounding on any mesh.
This verifies assembly, loading and reduction, not discretization error.
Finally, the displacement change caused by a seeded random load
perturbation is compared with the bound in Eq.~\eqref{eq:solvers-perturbation}.
