\section{Numerical methods and comparison protocol}
\label{sec:solvers}

Section~\ref{sec:mechanics-matrix} showed that $A$ is sparse, symmetric
and positive definite, with sensitivity measured by $\kappa_2(A)$. These
properties decide which solvers apply and how fast they converge. Let
$N=|F|$ be the number of unknowns, and $x_k$, $e_k=x-x_k$ and
$r_k=b-Ax_k$ the $k$-th iterate, error and residual.

A direct method factorizes $A$ as $PA=LU$ or, for symmetric positive
definite $A$, as $A=LL^T$ (Cholesky) at half the cost, then solves two
triangular systems~\cite{trefethen-bau}; forming $A^{-1}$ costs more and
is less accurate. Iterative methods avoid the extra nonzeros (fill-in)
of sparse factors and only multiply by $A$.

A stationary iteration corrects $x_k$ with the residual, using an easily
inverted approximation $M$ of $A$: $x_{k+1}=x_k+M^{-1}r_k$, so
$e_{k+1}=(I-M^{-1}A)\,e_k$. It therefore converges for every $x_0$ if
and only if the spectral radius $\rho(I-M^{-1}A)$ is below
one~\cite{saad-iterative}. Jacobi uses
$M=D_A$, the diagonal of $A$. Its iteration matrix is similar to $I-S$
with $S=D_A^{-1/2}AD_A^{-1/2}$, so
\begin{equation}
 \rho_J=\max_i\,|1-\lambda_i(S)|.
 \label{eq:solvers-jacobi-radius}
\end{equation}
As $\lambda_i(S)>0$, Jacobi converges if and only if
$\lambda_{\max}(S)<2$. Damped Jacobi, $M=D_A/\omega$, converges if and
only if $0<\omega<2/\lambda_{\max}(S)$; we use $\omega=0.5$. Gauss--Seidel
takes $M$ as the lower triangle of $A$ and converges for every symmetric
positive definite $A$~\cite{saad-iterative}.

The remaining methods exploit that $x$ minimizes
$\phi(y)=\tfrac12y^TAy-b^Ty$, the elastic energy of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:mechanics-energy} minus the work of the loads. Steepest
descent minimizes $\phi$ along $r_k=-\nabla\phi(x_k)$; each step
multiplies the energy norm of the error, $\|e\|_A=(e^TAe)^{1/2}$, by at
most $(\kappa-1)/(\kappa+1)$, where $\kappa=\kappa_2(A)$. The conjugate
gradient (CG) method instead minimizes $\|e_k\|_A$ over
$x_0+\mathcal{K}_k$, where 
$\mathcal{K}_k=\mathrm{span}\{r_0,Ar_0,\ldots,A^{k-1}r_0\}$ is the Krylov
space. It needs one product by $A$ per step and satisfies~\cite{shewchuk-cg}
\begin{equation}
 \|e_k\|_A\leq2\left(\frac{\sqrt\kappa-1}{\sqrt\kappa+1}\right)^k\|e_0\|_A,
 \label{eq:solvers-cg-bound}
\end{equation}
so its iteration count grows like $\sqrt\kappa$ rather than $\kappa$.
Diagonally preconditioned CG (PCG) is equivalent to CG applied to $S$,
so $\kappa_2(S)$ replaces $\kappa$;
it helps only if the diagonal of $A$ varies strongly.
MINRES minimizes $\|r_k\|_2$ over the same space~\cite{paige-saunders-minres}.
Full GMRES does so for any $A$ but stores all $k$ basis
vectors~\cite{saad-schultz-gmres}, so it shows the cost of ignoring symmetry.

\paragraph{Comparison protocol.} Every method receives the same $A$ and
$b$, starts from $x_0=0$ and is accepted
only if its recomputed relative residual satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \eta(\hat x)=\frac{\|b-A\hat x\|_2}{\|b\|_2}<10^{-8}.
 \label{eq:solvers-residual}
\end{equation}
Reaching the cap of 20{,}000 iterations, or $\eta>10^{10}$ for ordinary
Jacobi, counts as failure. Accuracy is the relative error
$\|\hat x-x_{\mathrm{ref}}\|_2/\|x_{\mathrm{ref}}\|_2$ against the sparse
LU solution $x_{\mathrm{ref}}$ of the same system~\cite{scipy-splu}. Times
are the best of three runs, excluding assembly and diagnostics. Extreme
eigenvalues, for $\kappa_2(A)$ and $\rho_J$, come from dense or, for large
systems, sparse eigensolvers~\cite{scipy-eigsh}.

Since $\hat x$ solves exactly the system with load perturbed by its
residual, the standard bound~\cite{trefethen-bau}
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\|\delta x\|_2}{\|x\|_2}\leq\kappa_2(A)\frac{\|\delta b\|_2}{\|b\|_2}
 \label{eq:solvers-perturbation}
\end{equation}
limits its relative error to $\kappa_2(A)\,\eta$. This
\emph{algebraic} error measures how well one mesh's equations are solved.
\emph{Discretization} error, between finite element and continuum
displacements, does not shrink with the tolerance; we estimate it by
comparing meshes at matched physical points.

Later sections also check that $d_C$ equals the prescribed values,
that applied loads and the reactions of Eq.~\eqref{eq:mechanics-reactions}
have zero resultant force and moment, and that the response to a random
load perturbation obeys Eq.~\eqref{eq:solvers-perturbation}. A patch
test pulls a plate held only by rollers (left edge fixed horizontally,
corner at the origin vertically) with uniform traction $p$. Its exact
solution $u_x=px/E$, $u_y=-\nu py/E$ is linear, so linear triangles must
reproduce it to rounding on any mesh, verifying assembly, loading and
reduction.
