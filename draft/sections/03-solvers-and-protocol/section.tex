\section{Numerical methods and comparison protocol}
\label{sec:solvers}

Section~\ref{sec:mechanics-matrix} showed that $A$ is sparse, symmetric
and positive definite, with sensitivity measured by $\kappa_2(A)$. These
properties decide which solvers apply and how fast they converge. Let
$N=|F|$ be the number of unknowns, $x_k$ the $k$-th iterate,
$e_k=x-x_k$ its error and $r_k=b-Ax_k$ its residual.

\subsection{Methods}
A direct method factorizes $A$ into triangular factors, $PA=LU$ or, since
$A$ is symmetric positive definite, Cholesky $A=LL^T$ at half the
cost~\cite{trefethen-bau}, and then solves by two triangular
substitutions. Forming $A^{-1}$ costs more and is less accurate. For
sparse $A$ the factors gain extra nonzeros (fill-in), so memory and work
grow faster than the number of nonzeros of $A$. Iterative methods
instead only multiply by $A$, which is cheap because $A$ is sparse.

A stationary iteration corrects $x_k$ with the residual, using an easily
inverted approximation $M$ of $A$:
\[
 x_{k+1}=x_k+M^{-1}r_k,\qquad e_{k+1}=(I-M^{-1}A)\,e_k.
\]
It therefore converges for every $x_0$ if and only if the spectral radius
$\rho(I-M^{-1}A)$ is below one~\cite{saad-iterative}. Jacobi uses
$M=D_A$, the diagonal of $A$. Its iteration matrix is similar to $I-S$
with $S=D_A^{-1/2}AD_A^{-1/2}$, so
\begin{equation}
 \rho_J=\max_i\,|1-\lambda_i(S)|.
 \label{eq:solvers-jacobi-radius}
\end{equation}
Positive definiteness gives only $\lambda_i(S)>0$, so Jacobi diverges
when $\lambda_{\max}(S)>2$.

Krylov methods build $x_k$ from the space
$\mathcal{K}_k=\mathrm{span}\{r_0,Ar_0,\ldots,A^{k-1}r_0\}$. For symmetric
positive definite $A$, solving $Ax=b$ is the same as minimizing the
energy $\phi(y)=\tfrac12y^TAy-b^Ty$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:mechanics-energy} minus
the work of the loads). The conjugate gradient (CG) method minimizes the
energy norm $\|e_k\|_A=(e_k^TAe_k)^{1/2}$ over $x_0+\mathcal{K}_k$ with one
product by $A$ per step, and~\cite{shewchuk-cg}
\begin{equation}
 \|e_k\|_A\leq2\left(\frac{\sqrt\kappa-1}{\sqrt\kappa+1}\right)^k\|e_0\|_A,
 \qquad \kappa=\kappa_2(A).
 \label{eq:solvers-cg-bound}
\end{equation}
Its iteration count therefore grows like $\sqrt\kappa$, whereas for
steepest descent it grows like $\kappa$. Table~\ref{tab:solvers-methods}
lists the eight iterative methods we compare.

\subsection{Comparison protocol}
Every method receives the same $A$ and $b$, starts from $x_0=0$ and is
accepted only if its recomputed relative residual satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \eta(\hat x)=\frac{\|b-A\hat x\|_2}{\|b\|_2}<10^{-8}.
 \label{eq:solvers-residual}
\end{equation}
Reaching the cap of 20{,}000 iterations, or $\eta>10^{10}$ for ordinary
Jacobi, counts as failure. Accuracy is the relative error
$\|\hat x-x_{\mathrm{ref}}\|_2/\|x_{\mathrm{ref}}\|_2$ against the sparse
LU solution $x_{\mathrm{ref}}$ of the same system~\cite{scipy-splu}. Times
are the best of three runs, excluding assembly and diagnostics. The
extreme eigenvalues behind $\kappa_2(A)$ and $\rho_J$ are computed densely
for small systems and with SciPy's \texttt{eigsh} for large
ones~\cite{scipy-eigsh}.

The computed $\hat x$ solves exactly the system whose load is perturbed by
its residual. The standard bound~\cite{trefethen-bau}
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\|\delta x\|_2}{\|x\|_2}\leq\kappa_2(A)\frac{\|\delta b\|_2}{\|b\|_2}
 \label{eq:solvers-perturbation}
\end{equation}
therefore limits its relative error to $\kappa_2(A)\,\eta$. This
\emph{algebraic} error measures how well one mesh's equations are solved.
\emph{Discretization} error, between finite element and continuum
displacements, does not shrink with the tolerance; we estimate it by
comparing meshes at matched physical points.

Finally, later sections check that the prescribed values $d_C$ are
reproduced; that applied loads and the reactions of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:mechanics-reactions} have zero resultant force and moment;
that the displacement change caused by a random load perturbation obeys
Eq.~\eqref{eq:solvers-perturbation}; and a patch test. In the patch test
the left edge is restrained only horizontally and the corner at the
origin vertically, and the opposite edge carries uniform traction $p$.
The exact plane-stress solution $u_x=px/E$, $u_y=-\nu py/E$ is linear,
so linear triangles must reproduce it to rounding on any mesh. This
verifies assembly, loading and reduction.
