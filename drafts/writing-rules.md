# Writing rules for the next sections

Apply these rules when drafting or revising our report, based on the team's
feedback on the opening section:

- Write connected paragraphs. Do not split ordinary explanations into bullet
  points. Use lists only for actual steps or items to compare.
- Use plain language and direct sentences. Explain technical terms when first
  needed; avoid dense jargon, awkward opening lists and unnecessary commas.
- Explain the purpose and physical meaning before introducing the mathematics.
  Give each paragraph one main idea and connect it to the next.
- Keep only equations needed to follow that section. Put detailed derivations
  in the relevant later section. Define every new symbol, subscript and matrix
  block where it appears. When relating equivalent systems, show them together:
  \(Ax=b\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad K_{FF}d_F=f_F-K_{FC}d_C\), then explain
  the correspondence in words.
- Add a useful picture when explaining geometry or triangulation. Refer to it
  in the text and explain what it shows in the caption. Identify illustrative
  meshes as examples; do not present them as computed project results.
- Describe mesh choices accurately. Nonuniform triangles are not inherently
  incorrect, but mesh size and quality can affect the approximation. Support
  accuracy claims with mesh comparisons rather than appearance alone.
- Keep the agreed scope and length. Remove repetition before tightening spacing
  or shrinking text. Cite sources and distinguish assumptions from results.
- Compile the exact revised source and inspect the PDF before claiming its
  layout is checked. Check figures, definitions, references and readability.

These rules guide future sections; they do not reopen the current introduction.
